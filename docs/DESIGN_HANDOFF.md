## What the demo is for

- Build a small, local macOS game for two friends sharing one keyboard. The fighter has no feet or independent walking control: the weapon is both the way to move and the way to fight.
- Test whether new players can deliberately move, repeat a swing, aim, and land a hit. An accidental win alone is not a successful playtest. Use the current controls, help panel, practice mode, HUD, sound, and hit feedback; a guided tutorial can come later.
- Try Tripo-generated 3D assets in the Electron + React + React Three Fiber game. Tripo makes assets outside the running game; the game loads local GLBs. The first polearm trial was appearance-only. The later sword trial is a separate weapon profile with shorter reach and faster swings.

## Decisions already made

- Latest beagle decision, 19 October: implement the normal-controls video and add a small hover to the separate skill now, using F. This supersedes the earlier hover deferral. The local implementation and controls are in [skill handoff](BEAGLE_SPIN_SKILL.md). The old flip extension below is historical and no longer drives gameplay.

- Historical flip experiment, now inactive: the approved smooth v05 rig received coupled body/ear rotation. W/S loaded the ears against the ground and powered front/back vaults; A/D steered throughout. A supported loading stance assisted takeoff, then body pitch and relative ear swing followed a coupled mass matrix with finite motor torque, gravity and damping. Rotated hurt shapes, sockets, collision velocities, fallback and GLB posing agreed. Release/reversal, shared stamina and ordinary weapon rules remained. The creator reported a lying-down result and set flips aside. See [flip handoff](BEAGLE_FLIPS.md). PR #1 saved the earlier upright baseline; the experiment was not pushed.

- The creator requested both fighters to retreat after a weapon clash, opposite each one's current heading. The local trial now applies a horizontal backward recoil on clear impacts, preserving lateral/vertical and faster backward motion. It requires separation and cooldown to repeat, resets on rematch, and respects arena bounds. Existing sound/particles accompany it; the rule adds no health damage or stamina cost. Strength remains for playtesting; see [control system](CONTROL_SYSTEM.md).
- After trying the published build, the creator approved testing 50% of the previous maximum swing and horizontal speeds for all three weapons, retaining acceleration/braking and initial damage/stamina settings. Local axis caps are sword 6.1, polearm 4.25, and hammer 3.4 rad/s, including after collisions. Keep visual size and reach. The trial adds measured sword-guard and polearm-blade proxies plus intermediate contact sampling; the fixed ground timestep remains. No extra forgiving block margin was approved for immediate use; assess it only if blocks remain too difficult. See [control system](CONTROL_SYSTEM.md) for limitations and [project status](PROJECT_STATUS.md) for checks.
- Combat now includes the creator's stamina choice: 200 points, 20/s for front/back swing, 8/s for horizontal steering, additive combined costs, and 30/s recovery after 0.5 seconds without weapon input. Below 25 points, powered strength and driven speed reduce toward 40% at zero, with normal reverse braking and passive coasting retained. Both duel views, practice, and solo share the rule. Health and stamina are labeled separately. The initial costs are shared across weapons; feel and balance remain for playtesting.
- The creator requested weaker horizontal-turn damage than vertical hits. Pure turning hits now use a 50% damage multiplier at equivalent impact conditions; mixed hits blend by the actual incoming contact motion, including after releasing input. Vertical and body-translation contributions retain full weighting, and normal physical collision/blocking impulses remain. See [control system](CONTROL_SYSTEM.md) for the calculation and regression coverage.
- The two-player duel now uses two side-by-side views **inside the same Electron window**, one independent solo-style free view for each fighter. They render the same match through two cameras; the physics loop advances once. Practice keeps the shared camera, and solo keeps its independent mouse camera.
- A separate **fixed-camera two-player duel** is available from the menu. It uses the original elevated shared camera and one full-width viewport. Both players use the current controls and weapon rules, and rematches retain the selected view mode. This is another way to view the same game.
- Keep the custom contact-driven movement. The creator approved the [control animation](control-concept-combinations.mp4): from overhead, W (↑) swings toward the fighter's front and S (↓) toward the back. Either input can continue past the downward pose so ground contact can lift the fighter and carry the weapon around the other side. Releasing preserves momentum; reverse input brakes first. This supersedes the earlier fixed up/down targets and ten-degree follow-through rule.
- A / D (← / →) change only the weapon's horizontal direction, and can be held with W or S. The fighter has no separate walking or turning input. The visible face follows weapon azimuth; the heading stays stable when the weapon points vertically. Both fighters now start with weapon heading aligned to facing; the polearm starts raised to avoid overlap.
- The creator approved the rear-view preview for both split-screen cameras: start behind each fighter with a small 15° heading offset, a shallow 0.35-radian orbit pitch, 55° field of view, 10.5-unit distance, and smooth weapon-relative composition. This framing shows the far arena edge while keeping the fighter readable. A / D and arrow-key weapon steering leave camera azimuth independent. P1 holds Q / E to orbit left / right; P2 uses period / slash (the ? key, without Shift). Both camera key pairs have been reversed following the creator's playtest feedback. Releasing keeps the angle, each camera is independent, and rematches restore the defaults. The duel has no mouse controls. Both panes show a direction indicator relative to their own view.
- Split-screen translation stays anchored to the fighter, and focus height follows jumps. A body/head framing check pulls back immediately when needed and eases inward afterward. This retains protection against losing the fighter during movement and narrow-window resizing. Split-screen now matches solo at three times the original arena radius (19.35 units), including the visual floor, physics boundary, rim contacts, and lighting. Starting marks remain aligned with fighters. Fixed-camera duel and practice retain the original arena; combat rules stay the same.
- Solo exploration has one fighter, a larger arena, and a mouse-controlled camera. A / D rotate the weapon; mouse drag rotates the view. The camera keeps the weapon in frame smoothly, and the view starts higher than the first solo version. The heading cue shows the fighter's facing and swing-plane direction relative to the view.
- The first Tripo model came from the supplied curved-blade polearm image. It is untextured; its color and finish can be revised later. The original trial used this same model for both players with color accents.
- The current local duel defaults to player one's shorter, faster sword versus player two's existing curved polearm model. The sword has no separate fighter travel-speed bonus; movement still comes from weapon contact. The armory displays actual models, descriptions, strengths, and tradeoffs. Both players select independently before a match, including matching weapons, and rematches retain both choices. Solo exposes only player one's choice. The sword's source is a public Tripo gallery model, optimized locally with its textures retained.
- The longer-term game is planned to have **multiple weapons with different gameplay effects**. The current sword is the first example, not the whole weapon system. The approved third weapon is now an iron hammer with slower response, broad head contacts, and 30% stronger head damage. Further weapon types, special effects, and final balance remain design work.
- The sword is intended to start level and point toward the opponent. Its model has been aligned with the collision segment, and a visual rotation change was made to keep the flat blade's facing continuous through the downward pose. These code changes have **not** resolved the creator's concern to their satisfaction.

## Open questions and observations

1. **Sword direction while standing on it:** The creator still sees a direction change that feels wrong when the sword is used to support movement. Reproduce the exact input sequence and compare the sword to the old polearm with the same camera angle. Determine whether the problem is the visible blade facing, the direction of the physics movement, the camera, or a combination. Do not mark this fixed based on the current code or screenshots alone.
2. **Continuous swing rule:** Check whether W and S feel like deliberate front/back choices from overhead, whether continued rotation through ground support is controllable, and whether combined W/S plus A/D input curves the path as expected. The earlier fixed-angle controller remains at Git checkpoint `8cc6441` for comparison.
3. **Camera and orientation cues:** The solo direction dial shows fighter facing and swing-plane heading. The split-screen framing safeguard has automated coverage for all four loadouts, independent camera input, angle retention, fast turns, vertical movement, shake, and narrow panes. Hands-on playtesting should still judge turning comfort and whether players can see enough of the moving weapon. Test whether a clearer support/contact cue is needed.
4. **Sword art:** The first public sword model looks unusual in motion to the creator. Decide whether local orientation/material adjustment is enough or whether to generate a new asset in Tripo. Do not spend more Tripo credits without discussing the intended design and cost. The local trial adds solid crossguard coverage around the existing shaft proxy; decorative flames remain excluded from additional coverage.
5. **Playtest and tuning:** Have two new players try a local match without live instruction. Note when movement, aiming, and hits are deliberate. If they struggle, consider easier control tuning after observing which actions fail. Check sword-versus-polearm balance only after the direction issue is understood.
6. **Later features:** A guided beginner tutorial remains an idea for a later version. Further camera tuning can follow a two-person playtest.
7. **Weapon roster:** The creator approved three contrasting roles: quick sword, long-reach polearm, and a slower heavy weapon, using the same four controls. They selected [this iron-head Tripo model](https://studio.tripo3d.ai/3d-model/heavy-fantasy-war-hammer-with-iron-head-wrapped-wooden-shaft-and-red-24462042-16ba-4905-a4db-a3bfe9a6500a) after reviewing its image. They approved 5 credits and it is now exported, optimized to 2.8 MB / ~80k triangles / 1K texture, and integrated. The native +Y shaft and butt were measured; five capsules cover the solid shaft/head while excluding cloth. The same yaw × pitch drives rendering and off-axis contacts. Both players select it in the armory. Its starting profile is slower with higher inertia and 30% more metal-head damage; final balance still needs playtesting. See [hammer trial](HAMMER_TRIAL.md).
8. **Contact and combat acceptance:** The local trial catches between-pose crossings, adds measured guard/blade contacts, and now makes both fighters recoil backward on clear clashes. Bounded sampling and rounded proxies still have limitations; assess blocks, recoil strength, and movement before calling contacts accepted. A 30% speed cap, extra block allowance, and clash pause/readout remain unimplemented. Body hits retain pause/readout; clashes have physical impulses, recoil, sound, and particles.
9. **Stamina acceptance:** Verify deliberate movement and defense when exhausted, recovery timing, and damage balance across all sixteen loadout pairings. Automated checks show these rules execute; they do not establish comfortable pacing or final numerical balance.
