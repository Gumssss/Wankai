| Motion | Player 1 | Player 2 | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Swing toward the fighter's front | W | ↑ | Rotates the weapon forward in its vertical plane. From overhead, it descends in front. |

W or S can be held with A or D. Swinging does not stop at an up or down angle target; the weapon can continue past the floor-facing pose while ground contact pushes the fighter. Opposite swing inputs brake before reversing. Releasing keys removes motor input but preserves momentum under gravity, damping, and collision. Pressing both opposite keys on the same axis cancels that axis's input.

Floating weapons use the selected profile's `motorAcceleration` and `maxAngularSpeed` on both axes; the beagle's normal steering and rotor controller are described below. Pitch reversal uses its `brakeAcceleration`; yaw reversal uses `motorAcceleration`. Released motion uses damping rather than a separate release brake. The approved half-speed trial retains acceleration/braking and clamps each axis to the new limit both before angle integration and after collision response. The proposed fourth weapon would need independent yaw tuning; it is not implemented.

## Stamina and directional damage

Each fighter has 200 stamina, independent of health. Effective front/back input costs 20 points per simulation second, horizontal steering costs 10, and simultaneous inputs add their costs. Held input still costs stamina when blocked or braking. Opposite keys cancel their axis before charging; camera controls, passive motion, and collisions are free. After both weapon axes are released for 0.6 seconds, stamina recovers at 24 points per second. Continued input at zero prevents recovery. Paid rotor time replaces ordinary input charging while active; deployment, spinning and retraction all block regeneration.

Below 60 stamina, powered acceleration and the driven speed target decrease linearly toward 40% of each weapon's normal values at zero. This includes the cat's steering target, normal long-ear steering and rotor speed/acceleration. Existing reverse braking strength is preserved. When driving in the current direction above the tired speed target, the motor brakes progressively using that weapon's braking acceleration; releasing input retains normal coasting. Gravity, ground support, and collision impulses continue to act. These rules apply to all weapons and modes. Countdown, pause, hit pause, and results freeze stamina with physics; rematches reset it. From empty, ordinary full power returns after 3.1 seconds of release.

At equal closing speed, acceleration, and contact surface, a pure yaw/turning hit has a 0.5 damage multiplier and a pure pitch/vertical swing retains full damage. Mixed hits blend the multiplier using the positive closing contributions of yaw, pitch, and relative body translation at the actual contact point. Body translation is neutral; motion tangential to the contact does not affect the blend. This uses pre-impact motion rather than held keys, so coasting turns remain weaker. The multiplier composes with the hammer head bonus and is rounded once after the base integer damage. Collision and blocking impulses retain their physical calculation; damage-related feedback and the additional hit knockback follow the final damage.

The HUD shows separate numeric stamina and character-specific health bars, low-stamina/exhaustion text and rotor readiness/cost. Basic Fighter has 100 HP; Beagle 80 HP, Yellow Blob 110 HP and Cat 150 HP. Dummy restoration, rematch and timeout comparisons use each fighter's own maximum. Shared tuning lives in `src/combat-settings.js`; directional damage remains `CONFIG.turnDamageScale` in `src/physics.js`. Automated coverage is in `tests/combat-stamina.test.js` and `tests/balance-revision.test.js`; final pacing and exhausted movement still need hands-on acceptance.

## Contact and feedback limits

The fixed simulation keeps 180 Hz motor, stamina, gravity, skill timers and ground integration. `src/combat-contacts.js` detects weapon clashes and body hits together along the motion between consecutive poses, resolves the earliest event, updates the remaining trajectory and checks it again. A movement bound using translation, angular travel and the thinnest capsule sets the number of samples, up to 64 intervals per search. The first sampled event is refined ten times. Heading interpolation takes the short path through angle wrap; ear deployment and lag also interpolate.

Redundant capsules are combined into physical contact patches. Independent approaching patches survive reduction, so a deep slow overlap cannot hide a separate fast strike. Damage uses one deterministic representative selected by incoming normal impulse, with the actual striking surface's damage scale. Simultaneous damage reads one common incoming state before any health or impulse results are applied. An earlier defeat prevents later damage, while a genuinely simultaneous lethal trade can draw. Impulses are solved together on working velocities in a canonical geometric order, rather than giving either player slot priority. Continuing contact across adjacent capsules receives no repeated restitution merely because the source capsule changes.

Physical contact and damage eligibility are separate. Harmless contact can become a qualifying impact without first separating. Each logical striking part (left/right paw or ear, or a whole ordinary weapon) tracks its own processed episode against the target; its full geometry must separate continuously for over 0.045 seconds to rearm. All parts share the existing directed attacker–target damage interval of over 0.28 seconds. Simultaneous qualifying parts yield one hit, and every qualifying part is marked processed even when cooldown suppresses its damage. A suppressed impact cannot queue damage for cooldown expiry, sustained contact cannot grind health, and a harmless parked part cannot disable another part.

Event times use simulation time. Additional contact searches do not repeat resource spending, motors, gravity or skill timers. Linear/angular limits apply before checking the revised remaining trajectory. After an impact changes motion, environmental constraints are restored without applying friction a second time. The same resolver serves every loadout and combat mode; the public single-pose attack/clash queries delegate to it without advancing motion. Rematch resets its contact episodes with the world. Contact events are delivered once through the existing session loop.

This catches the reproduced crossing even when both endpoint poses are separated, without adding collision radius. It is bounded pose sampling, not an analytic continuous collision solver: very shallow grazing contacts, extreme motion beyond the sample budget, and the capsule approximations remain limitations. A 30% speed cap and an extra weapon-block allowance have not been applied.

The sword retains its original shaft capsule and adds two measured crossguard capsules. The polearm retains its shaft and adds four collar/blade/tip/barb capsules. The hammer retains its five existing solid capsules. All solid endpoints participate in ground/rim contacts; model scale and reach are unchanged. The sword's flame wisps and hammer cloth do not receive extra proxies. These remain rounded approximations and can overestimate local thickness.

Measurements reproduce the renderer's source-AABB normalization and butt/axis transform. The additions improve measured sword vertex coverage from 94.18% to 99.08%, and polearm from 94.41% to 100%; vertex-weighted coverage does not prove exact collision geometry or final balance. `tests/contact-motion.test.js` covers positive crossings, nearby misses, added solid hits/blocks, heading wrap, limits, and ground support. The original sword support-direction complaint still needs hands-on acceptance.

`tests/combat-resolution.test.js` covers the three original failures, chronology, simultaneous lethal trades, paired-part episodes, suppressed impacts, redundant geometry, surface multipliers and once-per-tick resources. It also checks all 81 ordered loadout pairings in three varied starting states with player slots reversed. Approved yellow movement and pre-contact frames remain exact; post-contact trajectories from the older clash-first/player-first solver are intentionally superseded. Archived replay files and videos remain historical evidence.

Weapon clashes apply physical impulses. Clear impacts also make the pair separate along their horizontal centre-to-centre axis, independent of facing or camera. Each fighter receives at least 1.8–3.2 m/s outward velocity according to impact speed, preserving tangent/vertical motion and faster existing outward momentum. This replaces the previous facing-relative arcade recoil. The shared chronological solver applies it at the event time before checking remaining motion. Normal friction, collisions and arena boundaries then act on that motion.

`CLASH` in `src/physics.js` sets an impact threshold above 2.5 m/s, full recoil at 12 m/s, a 0.18-second event cooldown, and 0.05 seconds of weapon separation before rearming. A gentle touch or sustained overlap therefore does not repeatedly apply the extra recoil. Rematches reset these gates. Recoil itself adds no damage or stamina charge; existing weapon deflection and half-speed limits remain.

The same clear impact emits the existing sound/particles. Body hits additionally trigger hit pause, damage numbers, a readout, and damage-based knockback. Extra clash pause/readout remains a proposal. `tests/clash-recoil.test.js` checks both heading directions, momentum components, gentle/sustained contacts, separation/cooldown, arena bounds, and rematch reset. Recoil strength still needs hands-on acceptance.
